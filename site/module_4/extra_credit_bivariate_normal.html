Extra Credit: Height, Weight, and the Bivariate Normal Model

The problem: predicting weight from height

Suppose we want to predict a man’s weight from his height. Let \(H\) be height in inches and \(W\) be weight in pounds. Rather than model \(H\) and \(W\) separately, we can model the pair \((H,W)\) jointly with a bivariate normal distribution.

For U.S. adult men, realistic values are approximately

$$ \mu_H = 69 \text{ inches}, \qquad \sigma_H = 3 \text{ inches}, $$

and

$$ \mu_W = 198 \text{ pounds}, \qquad \sigma_W = 35 \text{ pounds}. $$

Height and weight are positively associated, but the strength of that association is not determined by the two marginal means and standard deviations. We therefore include a correlation parameter \(\rho\), which is unknown. The general bivariate normal density is

$$ f_{H,W}(h,w) = \frac{1}{2\pi\sigma_H\sigma_W\sqrt{1-\rho^2}} \exp\left[ -\frac{1}{2(1-\rho^2)} \left( \frac{(h-\mu_H)^2}{\sigma_H^2} - 2\rho\frac{(h-\mu_H)(w-\mu_W)}{\sigma_H\sigma_W} + \frac{(w-\mu_W)^2}{\sigma_W^2} \right) \right]. $$

The unknown parameter \(\rho\) controls how strongly height and weight move together.

Simplifying by standardizing

To simplify the algebra, standardize height and weight:

$$ X=\frac{H-69}{3}, \qquad Y=\frac{W-198}{35}. $$

Then \(X\) and \(Y\) have means \(0\) and variances \(1\), and the pair \((X,Y)\) is still bivariate normal. Standardizing does not change correlation, so

$$ \operatorname{Cor}(X,Y)=\operatorname{Cor}(H,W)=\rho. $$

With means \(0\) and variances \(1\), the joint density simplifies to

$$ f_{X,Y}(x,y) = \frac{1}{2\pi\sqrt{1-\rho^2}} \exp\left[ -\frac{x^2-2\rho xy+y^2}{2(1-\rho^2)} \right], \qquad -\infty<x<\infty,\; -\infty<y<\infty. $$

Here \(X\) and \(Y\) are measured in standard deviations. For example, \(X=1.5\) corresponds to a height of

$$ 69+3(1.5)=73.5 \text{ inches}. $$

The correlation parameter \(\rho\) remains unknown. In the problems below, you will derive the marginal distribution of \(X\), the conditional distribution of \(Y\) given \(X\), and the correlation of \(X\) and \(Y\).

Extra credit opportunity

The following three parts are each worth one point of extra credit on a Module Quiz. Show your derivation for each part.

Part A (1 point)

Derive the marginal distribution \(f_X(x)\) from the joint density \(f_{X,Y}(x,y)\).

Hint. Integrate the joint density over \(y\):

$$ f_X(x)=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty} f_{X,Y}(x,y)\,dy. $$

In the exponent, isolate the terms involving \(y\) and complete the square in \(y\). Then use the Gaussian integral

$$ \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} e^{-t^2/2}\,dt=\sqrt{2\pi}. $$

Part B (1 point)

Derive the conditional distribution \(f_{Y\mid X}(y\mid x)\). Then find \(E[Y\mid X=1.5]\) when \(\rho=0.65\).

Hint. Use

$$ f_{Y\mid X}(y\mid x)=\frac{f_{X,Y}(x,y)}{f_X(x)}. $$

Simplify the resulting exponent and rewrite the conditional density as a normal density. From that density, determine \(E[Y\mid X=x]\), then evaluate it at \(x=1.5\) when \(\rho=0.65\).

Part C (1 point)

Show that \(\operatorname{Cor}(X,Y)=\rho\).

Hint. The marginal distributions are standard normal, so \(\operatorname{Var}[X]=\operatorname{Var}[Y]=1\) and

$$ \operatorname{Cov}(X,Y)=E[XY]-E[X]E[Y]=E[XY]. $$

Represent the joint density as the conditional density from Part B times the marginal density from Part A:

$$ f_{X,Y}(x,y)=f_{Y\mid X}(y\mid x)f_X(x). $$

Then rewrite the cross-moment as a nested expectation:

$$ E[XY] = \int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty} xy\,f_{Y\mid X}(y\mid x)f_X(x)\,dy\,dx = \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} x\left(\int_{-\infty}^{\infty} y\,f_{Y\mid X}(y\mid x)\,dy\right)f_X(x)\,dx. $$
