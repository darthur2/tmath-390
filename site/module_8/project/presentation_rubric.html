Final Presentation: Guidelines and Rubric

Purpose

The final presentation gives you a chance to summarize the full project and communicate the most important results and conclusions. Your presentation should show that you can describe your data, summarize variables, fit statistical models, construct and interpret confidence intervals, and interpret a simple linear regression model in context. The emphasis is on clear communication, correct interpretation, and well-chosen visuals rather than on covering every detail of the written report.

Format

You may prepare your slides using either:

  • PowerPoint or another slide program.
  • R Markdown or Quarto, rendered to slides.

Your presentation should be 7 to 10 minutes long. Include one content slide for each of the seven required items below. You may add a title slide and a short conclusion slide, but the seven required slides should each be present and clearly identifiable.

You must also upload an audio-only recording of yourself presenting the slides. The recording should run 7 to 10 minutes and should accompany the slides and, when applicable, the R Markdown source.

Required slides

1. Dataset and variables

Provide an overview of the dataset and the variables used in the project. Explain what the dataset describes, what one row represents, and the context or population when those details are available. Identify the key variables and whether they are quantitative or categorical.

2. Univariate summary and visualization

Choose one variable and present a univariate numeric summary along with an accompanying visualization. Use summaries and a plot that fit the variable. Comment on the distribution, including center, spread, shape, and any interesting features such as skewness, outliers, or multiple modes.

3. Bivariate summary and visualization

Choose a pair of variables and present a bivariate numeric summary along with an accompanying visualization. Use a correlation and a scatterplot when both variables are quantitative. Comment on the direction, form, and strength of the relationship shown in the plot and summary.

4. Univariate model, parameters, and fitting

Show a univariate plot of a variable along with a fitted model overlaid on the plot. State which model you used for that variable and report the estimated parameters. Briefly explain why you chose that model and how the model was fit, including how the maximum likelihood estimate was obtained.

5. Confidence interval for a population mean

Present a confidence interval for a population mean for one of the variables. Interpret the interval in context, including the confidence level, the population, and the units.

6. Confidence interval for a difference in means

Present a confidence interval for a difference between two population means. Interpret the interval in context, including the direction of the difference, the units, and what the interval says about whether a difference is plausible.

7. Simple linear regression

Present a scatterplot with the fitted regression line and the regression equation. Include a 95% confidence interval for the slope parameter and interpret both the slope and the interval in context. Report \(R^2\) and interpret it in context as a measure of model fit. Also mention whether you found any violations of the regression assumptions. You do not need to show the diagnostic plots on this slide; a brief verbal summary of any concerns is sufficient.

Building slides with R Markdown

If you choose to make your slides with R Markdown:

  1. In RStudio, choose File → New File → R Markdown… → Presentation.
  2. Choose a presentation format such as ioslides_presentation or slidy_presentation for HTML slides, or beamer_presentation for PDF slides. Quarto’s revealjs format is also acceptable.
  3. Use ## headings for individual slide titles, bullet points for the content, and code chunks to produce plots and tables.
  4. Knit or render the file to HTML or PDF.
  5. Submit the rendered slides, the .Rmd source file, and your audio recording.

Submission

Submit the following:

  • PowerPoint option: the .pptx file, or a PDF export of the slides.
  • R Markdown option: the rendered HTML or PDF slides and the .Rmd source file.
  • Audio recording: an audio-only recording of you presenting the slides. The recording should run 7 to 10 minutes.

Your slides should be readable, your plots should be clearly labeled, and your interpretations should be stated in context. Do not simply read the slides aloud; use them to support your explanation. If you use the presentation template, replace all placeholders with your own content and results before submitting.

Submission checklist

  • All seven required content slides are present.
  • The slides are uploaded as a .pptx file, a PDF, or rendered R Markdown slides.
  • The .Rmd source file is uploaded when the R Markdown option is used.
  • The audio-only recording is uploaded and runs 7 to 10 minutes.
  • Plots are clearly labeled, and the interpretations are stated in context.

General Expectations

  • Include exactly one content slide for each of the seven required items. A title slide and a short conclusion slide are optional.
  • Upload the audio recording along with the slides and the R Markdown source file, when applicable.
  • Keep slides readable: use large enough text, clear labels, and plots that are not overcrowded.
  • Interpret results in context rather than simply reading numbers from the slides.
  • Use confidence intervals to describe plausible values and to discuss significance where appropriate.
  • For the regression slide, mention any assumption violations even if you do not show the diagnostic plots.
  • If you use R Markdown, make sure the source file reproduces the figures and results shown in the slides.
  • If you use the presentation template, replace every placeholder with your own content and results; do not submit the template unchanged.
