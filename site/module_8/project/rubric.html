Rubric

Simple Linear Regression Mini-Project Rubric

Total: 100 points

Criterion Points Full Credit Partial Credit Low / No Credit
SLR model setup and notation in context 20 The simple linear regression model is set up correctly, and the notation is explained clearly in context. The student identifies the response variable, predictor variable, intercept, slope, and error term, and gives a sensible description of what each means for the data at hand. The student also comments on whether a linear model appears reasonable. The model is mostly set up correctly, but some notation is unclear, incomplete, or only partly explained in context. The linearity check is present but not fully justified or described clearly. The model setup is incorrect or missing, notation is not explained in context, or there is no meaningful attempt to assess whether a linear model is reasonable.
Fit the model correctly 15 The regression model is fit correctly, and the parameter estimates are reported clearly. The fitted equation matches the output from the model, and the estimates are connected to the variables in context. The model is fit mostly correctly, but there are minor errors, omissions, or unclear reporting of the parameter estimates or fitted equation. The model fit is incorrect, incomplete, or not clearly reported.
Interpret beta_1 in context 15 The slope parameter beta_1 is interpreted correctly in context as the expected change in the response for a one-unit increase in the predictor. The interpretation is mostly correct, but it is somewhat vague, slightly inaccurate, or not fully tied to the context of the data. The interpretation is incorrect, missing, or not stated in context.
Confidence interval for beta_1 and significance discussion 15 A confidence interval for beta_1 is computed correctly and used appropriately to discuss whether the slope is statistically significant. The interval is interpreted correctly in context. The confidence interval is mostly correct, but the interpretation or significance discussion is incomplete, unclear, or only partly connected to the context. The interval is incorrect, missing, or not interpreted in context, and the discussion of significance is not meaningful.
Prediction at a given x value and prediction interval 20 The predicted response at the specified predictor value is computed correctly, and a prediction interval is given correctly. The student clearly distinguishes the prediction interval from a confidence interval and interprets it in context. The prediction or prediction interval is mostly correct, but the interpretation is incomplete, slightly unclear, or does not clearly distinguish prediction from estimation. The prediction or prediction interval is incorrect, missing, or not interpreted in context.
Coefficient of determination and interpretation 15 The coefficient of determination is reported correctly and interpreted correctly in context as the proportion of variability in the response explained by the linear model. The coefficient of determination is mostly correct, but the interpretation is vague, slightly inaccurate, or not fully tied to the context. The coefficient of determination is missing, incorrect, or not interpreted meaningfully in context.

General Expectations

  • Exploration beyond the required analysis is welcome, especially when the data raise interesting questions or reveal unexpected patterns.
  • Work should be organized, readable, and complete.
  • Notation should be explained in context, not just written as symbols.
  • Students should distinguish between interpretation of the fitted line, a confidence interval, and a prediction interval.
  • The linearity check should be based on the pattern in the data and used to judge whether SLR is reasonable.
  • Interpretations of statistical output should be stated in terms of the variables in the project.
