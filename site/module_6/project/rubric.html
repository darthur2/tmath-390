Rubric

Confidence Intervals Mini-Project Rubric

Total: 100 points

Criterion Points Full Credit Partial Credit Low / No Credit
Confidence interval for a population mean 45 The parameter is clearly stated in context, and the confidence level is identified. The student correctly uses a one-sample t interval, explains why the procedure is appropriate, and addresses the relevant assumptions. The interval is computed correctly and interpreted correctly in context. Most of the setup is correct, but one or more of the following are incomplete or slightly unclear: parameter statement, confidence level, assumption discussion, interval computation, or contextual interpretation. The student shows generally appropriate use of a t interval, but the justification or interpretation is not fully developed. The parameter is unclear or missing, the t method is used incorrectly, assumptions are not addressed, the interval is computed incorrectly, or the interpretation is not in context.
Confidence interval for a difference between population means 55 The parameter is clearly stated in context, and the confidence level is identified. The student correctly uses a two-sample t interval, explains why that procedure is appropriate, and justifies the equal-variance or unequal-variance choice. The interval is computed correctly and interpreted correctly in context. Most of the setup is correct, but the choice of equal vs. unequal variance, the assumption discussion, the computation, or the contextual interpretation is incomplete or only partly justified. The student shows generally correct understanding of the two-sample t interval, but some important detail is missing or unclear. The parameter is unclear or missing, the procedure is chosen incorrectly, the equal/unequal variance choice is not justified, assumptions are not addressed, the interval is computed incorrectly, or the interpretation is not in context.

General Expectations

  • Exploration beyond the required analysis is welcome, especially when the data raise interesting questions or reveal unexpected patterns.
  • Work should be organized, readable, and complete.
  • Parameter statements should be written in context, not just as symbols.
  • Confidence intervals should be interpreted in terms of the population quantity being estimated.
  • Assumptions should be discussed rather than merely stated.
  • Students should show that the chosen procedure is appropriate for the data and the question being asked.
