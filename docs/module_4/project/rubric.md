## Distributions and Statistical Models Mini-Project Rubric

Total: 100 points

| Criterion                                           | Points | Full Credit                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                     | Partial Credit                                                                                                                                                                                                                          | Low / No Credit                                                                                                                                                                               |
| --------------------------------------------------- | -----: | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Distribution choice and justification               |     45 | For each variable, a reasonable statistical model/distribution is chosen and clearly justified. The choice reflects the variable type and observed features of the data. Clearly identifiable cases are handled correctly, such as Bernoulli/binomial for success-failure data. | Most variables are matched to reasonable distributions, but some choices or justifications are incomplete, weak, or slightly mismatched to the data. The student shows some awareness of variable type and shape, but not consistently. | Distribution choices are often inappropriate, unsupported, or missing. Clearly identifiable cases are handled incorrectly, or the response does not show meaningful reasoning about the data. |

Parameter values are clearly stated and matched to the corresponding model for each variable.                                                                                                 | Most parameter estimates are correct, but there are minor errors, omissions, or unclear connections between the estimates and the selected distributions.                                                                               | Parameter estimation is largely incorrect, incomplete, or not connected to the chosen distributions.                                                                                          |
| Visual comparison of fitted vs. actual distribution |     30 | The fitted distribution is compared visually to the actual data in a clear and appropriate way. The student uses the comparison to explain how well the proposed model matches the empirical distribution and notes important differences when they appear.                     | The visual comparison is mostly appropriate, but the explanation is incomplete, somewhat vague, or misses some relevant features of the displayed distributions.                                                                        | The comparison is weak, unclear, or missing, or the student does not meaningfully interpret how the fitted distribution compares to the data.                                                 |

### General Expectations

- Exploration beyond the required analysis is welcome, especially when the data raise interesting questions or reveal unexpected patterns.
- Work should be organized, readable, and complete.
- Distribution choices should reflect the type of variable and the observed features of the data.
- When there is a clearly correct model family, correctness matters strongly.
- When there is not one obvious choice, the quality of the justification matters more.
- Visual comparisons should be used to support the model choice, not replace it.
