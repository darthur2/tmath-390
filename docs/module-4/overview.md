# Module 4 Overview

## Why This Module Matters

Module 4 is about distributions and statistical models: ways to describe uncertainty, answer probability questions, and summarize how random variables behave. Module 3 introduced the rules of probability; now we use those rules to model numerical outcomes. Whether we are counting defects, estimating a waiting time, or studying how two measurements vary together, a useful distribution helps us calculate probabilities, describe typical values and spread, and understand relationships between variables. These ideas support the statistical modeling and inference that come later in the course.

## What You'll Learn

You will learn how a **random variable** maps uncertain outcomes to numbers and how its **support**, **probability mass function (PMF)**, **probability density function (PDF)**, and **cumulative distribution function (CDF)** describe its possible values and probabilities. You will work with common discrete and continuous distributions, including Bernoulli, binomial, multinomial, Poisson, geometric, negative binomial, exponential, Gamma, Beta, Normal, and log-normal models. You will use **expectation** and **variance** to describe a variable's average and spread.

You will also study how **linear** and **nonlinear transformations** change a distribution, as well as how joint models describe two variables together. From a joint model, you will find **marginal** and **conditional distributions**, calculate **conditional expectations**, and use **covariance** and **correlation** to summarize associations. Throughout the module, R provides ways to calculate probabilities, moments, and relationships from these models.

## By the End of This Module, You Should Be Able To

After completing this module, you should be able to work with a probability model and do several things with confidence:

- Describe a random variable's support and distinguish its PMF, PDF, and CDF.
- Choose an appropriate discrete or continuous distribution for a scenario or observed data.
- Calculate probabilities, expectations, and variances from a distribution, using R when helpful.
- Determine how a monotonic, one-to-one transformation changes a random variable's distribution, expectation, and variance.
- Find marginal and conditional distributions from a joint PMF or PDF, and calculate conditional expectations.
- Calculate and interpret covariance and correlation from a joint distribution.
