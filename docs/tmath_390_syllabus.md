## Course Description

Investigates probability and statistics using exploratory data analysis and interactive computing. Study topics including conditional probability, independence, random variables, distribution functions, descriptive statistics, transformations, sampling errors, confidence intervals, least squares, and maximum likelihood.

## Learning Objectives

- Become proficient in performing exploratory data analysis using statistical software package R.
- Be able to compute and understand summary statistics.
- Be able to produce various graphs summarizing data.
- Become familiar with basic concepts in probability theory.
- Become familiar with important discrete/continuous distributions.
- Be able to choose appropriate analyses suited for the data at hand.
- Be able to construct confidence intervals for various types of parameters.
- Be able to obtain estimates of parameters for statistical models using maximum likelihood estimation.
- Perform and interpret simple linear regression, interpret coefficient of determination and correlation coefficient

## Use of AI

AI is absolutely amazing and I'm fully aware that you could use it to get almost 100% in the course if you used it to do everything. Unfortunately, the point of this course is for you to demonstrate what you know and not what AI knows. I want you to be in a position in life where you can use AI to do things more quickly and efficiently that you already know how to do well yourself. I don't want you to be in a position where you are using AI to do things you don't know how to do yourself, because in that situation, why not just have the AI do it in place of you.

Because of this, general use of AI for the course is discouraged. Students will not be allowed to use AI during class exercises, quizzes, or exams. AI should also not be used to complete data challenges although they may be used to assist you in certain ways. Some good ways in which AI could be used would include the following:

- Getting additional insights into topics learned in class.
- Generating new practice problems to test your knowledge that are based on ones used in class.
- Understanding what code is doing and how to write code to solve problems.
- Understanding why you got a problem wrong on a quiz or a practice quiz.

Some ways in which AI should not be used include:

- Writing code completely for you when working on a data challenge, quiz problem, or exercise.
- Answering questions on quizzes, exercises, or data challenges.

In general, if you are about to copy and paste output from AI for something, that is probably a sign you are not using it how it should be used in this course.

### Plagiarism

A major part of your experience at UW Tacoma will be reading, synthesizing, and using the knowledge and ideas of others. To plagiarize is to use the ideas––or unique phrasing of those ideas––without acknowledging that they originate from someone or someplace other than you. Attributing where you get your information builds your own authority to speak on that topic and provides valuable backing to the arguments you make. Attribution also distinguishes your ideas and words from those of others who came before you. At the University of Washington, plagiarism is a violation of the student conduct code, and the consequences can be serious. Though citing, quoting, and paraphrasing can be confusing at first, it is essential for your success at UWT that you familiarize yourself with these important conventions of academic writing. Additionally, plagiarism can be understood differently in various disciplines. For instance, the ways in which one summarizes others' ideas in texts, or attributes information from texts in one's own paper, are not the same in the sciences as they are in the humanities, or the social sciences. This means it is vital that you understand the specific expectations and guidelines for writing that will help you avoid plagiarizing in this class. If you have questions about what amounts to plagiarism, you are strongly encouraged to seek guidance from faculty and the Teaching and Learning Center as soon as possible. For more information, please see:
