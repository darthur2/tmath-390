How Strongly Do Two Variables Move Together?

At a community sustainability event, organizers record whether each visitor arrives by bike and whether they bring a reusable cup. They notice that the choices often occur together. How strongly are these two choices associated? A numerical summary could help organizers understand whether the same outreach is reaching people who make several lower-waste choices.

A small online shop compares how much of a product page visitors read with whether they make a purchase. Visitors who read more details seem more likely to buy. How strongly are page engagement and purchase associated? A numerical summary could help the shop evaluate product pages.

In both problems, we need a joint distribution for the pair. From it, we can find the marginal means and variances and the cross-moment, then calculate covariance and correlation.

From a joint model to correlation

For discrete variables with joint PMF \(P_{X,Y}(x,y)\), the marginal PMFs and cross-moment are

$$ P_X(x)=\sum_y P_{X,Y}(x,y),\qquad P_Y(y)=\sum_x P_{X,Y}(x,y),\qquad E[XY]=\sum_x\sum_y xy\,P_{X,Y}(x,y). $$

For continuous variables with joint density \(f_{X,Y}(x,y)\), the marginal PDFs and cross-moment are found by integration:

$$ f_X(x)=\int f_{X,Y}(x,y)\,dy,\qquad f_Y(y)=\int f_{X,Y}(x,y)\,dx,\qquad E[XY]=\int\!\int xy f_{X,Y}(x,y)\,dx\,dy. $$

If \(X\) is continuous and \(Y\) is discrete, the joint model is a density-mass function. Sum over \(Y\) to obtain \(f_X\), and integrate over \(X\) to obtain \(P_Y\):

$$ f_X(x)=\sum_y f_{X,Y}(x,y),\qquad P_Y(y)=\int f_{X,Y}(x,y)\,dx,\qquad E[XY]=\sum_y\int xy f_{X,Y}(x,y)\,dx. $$

Once the means, variances, and cross-moment are known, covariance and correlation are

$$ \operatorname{Cov}(X,Y)=E[XY]-E[X]E[Y], \qquad \rho_{X,Y}=\operatorname{Corr}(X,Y) =\frac{\operatorname{Cov}(X,Y)}{\sqrt{\operatorname{Var}[X]\operatorname{Var}[Y]}}. $$

For a continuous example, take \(f_{X,Y}(x,y)=x+y\) on \((0,1)^2\). Its marginal densities are \(f_X(x)=x+\frac12\) and \(f_Y(y)=y+\frac12\), so

$$ E[X]=E[Y]=\frac{7}{12},\qquad E[X^2]=E[Y^2]=\frac{5}{12},\qquad \operatorname{Var}[X]=\operatorname{Var}[Y]=\frac{11}{144}. $$

The cross-moment is \(E[XY]=\frac13\), giving \(\operatorname{Cov}(X,Y)=-\frac{1}{144}\) and \(\operatorname{Corr}(X,Y)=-\frac{1}{11}\). R can calculate this correlation using the same marginal and cross-moment formulas:

library(cubature)

joint_pdf <- function(x, y) x + y
# Integrating the joint PDF over the other variable gives these marginals.
marginal_x <- function(x) x + 1/2
marginal_y <- function(y) y + 1/2

mean_x <- integrate(function(x) x * marginal_x(x),
                     lower = 0, upper = 1)$value
mean_y <- integrate(function(y) y * marginal_y(y),
                     lower = 0, upper = 1)$value
second_x <- integrate(function(x) x^2 * marginal_x(x),
                       lower = 0, upper = 1)$value
second_y <- integrate(function(y) y^2 * marginal_y(y),
                       lower = 0, upper = 1)$value
mean_xy <- hcubature(
  function(v) v[1] * v[2] * joint_pdf(v[1], v[2]),
  lowerLimit = c(0, 0),
  upperLimit = c(1, 1)
)$integral

variance_x <- second_x - mean_x^2
variance_y <- second_y - mean_y^2
covariance_xy <- mean_xy - mean_x * mean_y
correlation_xy <- covariance_xy / sqrt(variance_x * variance_y)
correlation_xy
[1] -0.09090909

Covariance describes whether the variables tend to be above or below their means together, but its size depends on their units. Correlation standardizes covariance and lies between \(-1\) and \(1\). Independence of random variables means that the joint model factors into the product of the marginals: \(P_{X,Y}(x,y)=P_X(x)P_Y(y)\) for discrete variables, or \(f_{X,Y}(x,y)=f_X(x)f_Y(y)\) for continuous variables. Independence implies zero correlation when the variances are finite, but zero correlation alone does not generally imply independence.

Bike commuting and reusable cups: a simple joint PMF

Let \(X=1\) if a visitor arrived by bike and \(X=0\) otherwise. Let \(Y=1\) if the visitor brought a reusable cup and \(Y=0\) otherwise. All four combinations occur, and event records suggest that the two choices often occur together. A simple custom joint PMF representing that pattern is

$$ P_{X,Y}(x,y)= \begin{cases} \dfrac{3}{8},&(x,y)=(0,0)\text{ or }(1,1),\\ \dfrac{1}{8},&(x,y)=(0,1)\text{ or }(1,0),\\ 0,&\text{otherwise}. \end{cases} $$

The support is the full product \(\{0,1\}\times\{0,1\}\). Summing the joint PMF over the other variable gives

$$ P_X(0)=P_X(1)=\frac12, \qquad P_Y(0)=P_Y(1)=\frac12. $$

Find the means and second moments from these marginal PMFs:

$$ E[X]=E[Y]=\frac12, \qquad E[X^2]=E[Y^2]=\frac12. $$

Therefore,

$$ \operatorname{Var}[X]=\operatorname{Var}[Y] =\frac12-\left(\frac12\right)^2 =\frac14. $$

The cross-moment comes directly from the joint PMF. Only the outcome \((1,1)\) contributes a nonzero value:

$$ E[XY]=\sum_x\sum_y xy\,P_{X,Y}(x,y)=\frac38. $$

Thus

$$ \operatorname{Cov}(X,Y)=\frac38-\frac12\cdot\frac12=\frac18, \qquad \operatorname{Corr}(X,Y)=\frac{1/8}{\sqrt{(1/4)(1/4)}}=0.5. $$

In this model, visitors who arrive by bike are more likely to bring a reusable cup, and the two choices have a moderate positive correlation.

R can calculate the marginal distributions, means, variances, and correlation from the joint PMF:

x <- c(0, 1)
y <- c(0, 1)
joint <- matrix(c(3/8, 1/8,
                  1/8, 3/8), nrow = 2, byrow = TRUE)

px <- rowSums(joint)
py <- colSums(joint)
mean_x <- sum(x * px)
mean_y <- sum(y * py)
variance_x <- sum((x - mean_x)^2 * px)
variance_y <- sum((y - mean_y)^2 * py)
mean_xy <- sum(outer(x, y, "*") * joint)
covariance_xy <- mean_xy - mean_x * mean_y
correlation_xy <- covariance_xy / sqrt(variance_x * variance_y)
correlation_xy
[1] 0.5

Page engagement and purchases: a mixed joint model

Let \(X\) be the fraction of a product page a visitor reads, measured on a scale from \(0\) to \(1\), and let \(Y=1\) if the visitor purchases the product and \(Y=0\) otherwise. The engagement measurements are continuous and tend to be higher, while the outcome is a yes/no purchase. As a simple model, use a Beta\((2,1)\) marginal density for engagement, \(f_X(x)=2x\), and let the purchase probability given engagement \(x\) be \(P(Y=1\mid X=x)=x\).

Multiplying the marginal density by the conditional probability gives the joint density-mass function:

$$ f_{X,Y}(x,y)= \begin{cases} 2x^2,&0<x<1,\;y=1,\\ 2x(1-x),&0<x<1,\;y=0,\\ 0,&\text{otherwise}. \end{cases} $$

The support is the full product \((0,1)\times\{0,1\}\). Sum over the two purchase outcomes to recover the marginal density of engagement:

$$ f_X(x)=f_{X,Y}(x,0)+f_{X,Y}(x,1) =2x(1-x)+2x^2=2x, \qquad 0<x<1. $$

Integrate over engagement to find the purchase PMF:

$$ P_Y(1)=\int_0^1 2x^2\,dx=\frac23, \qquad P_Y(0)=\int_0^1 2x(1-x)\,dx=\frac13. $$

Calculate the marginal moments directly. For page engagement,

$$ E[X]=\int_0^1 x(2x)\,dx=\frac23, \qquad E[X^2]=\int_0^1 x^2(2x)\,dx=\frac12, $$

so \(\operatorname{Var}[X]=\frac12-(\frac23)^2=\frac1{18}\). For purchase status,

$$ E[Y]=\frac23,\qquad E[Y^2]=\frac23, \qquad \operatorname{Var}[Y]=\frac23-\left(\frac23\right)^2=\frac29. $$

Only purchases (\(Y=1\)) contribute to the cross-moment:

$$ E[XY]=\int_0^1 x\,f_{X,Y}(x,1)\,dx =\int_0^1 2x^3\,dx=\frac12. $$

The covariance and correlation are therefore

$$ \operatorname{Cov}(X,Y)=\frac12-\frac23\cdot\frac23=\frac1{18}, \qquad \operatorname{Corr}(X,Y)=\frac{1/18}{\sqrt{(1/18)(2/9)}}=\frac12. $$

This model describes a positive association: visitors who read more of the product page are more likely to buy it.

The pattern

Start with a joint PMF or PDF for the pair. Find each marginal distribution, calculate the marginal means and variances from their definitions, and evaluate \(E[XY]\) from the joint model. Covariance and correlation then summarize the direction and strength of the linear association.
