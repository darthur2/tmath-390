Module 1 Overview

How a small group of people can tell us something true about a very large one.

1  Why This Module Matters

Module 1 introduces the basic ideas that make statistics useful in real situations. The common thread is simple: whenever we want to learn about a bigger group, we usually have to work with a smaller group, and that means we need to think carefully about how questions are asked, how data are collected, and what the results really mean. The topics in this module matter because they are the foundation for making sound conclusions from data instead of guessing from a few examples or drawing conclusions from a biased sample.

2  What You'll Learn

By the end of this module, you will understand how statisticians move from a research question to a conclusion about a population. You will learn how to identify populations, samples, sampling frames, parameters, and statistics. You will also learn the difference between descriptive statistics and inferential statistics, how to recognize common sampling methods, and how to spot problems such as undercoverage, self-selection, and non-response. In addition, you will learn the difference between observational studies and experiments, including why completely randomized designs are important when researchers want to study causality.

3  By the End of This Module, You Should Be Able To

After completing this module, you should be able to read a statistical scenario and do several things with confidence:

  • Identify the target population, describe the sample and sampling frame, determine whether the study is observational or experimental, and recognize the sampling method being used.
  • Tell whether a statistic matches the parameter being studied.
  • Decide whether a conclusion is descriptive or inferential.
  • Explain when a study can support an association versus when it can support a causal claim.
  • Notice when a study design or sampling plan is weak enough to make the results less trustworthy.

4  What's Inside

  • Readings — three short stories that introduce the vocabulary in context: How Much Sugar Does the Average Person in the U.S. Consume?, How Much Do People Spend on Streaming Services?, and Does the Amount of TV You Watch Affect Your Performance in School?
  • Definitions and Examples — a glossary of every key term with sources from Mathematical Statistics with Applications in R and Introductory Statistics 2e.
  • Knowledge Checks — short practice questions that test each concept as you go.
  • Quiz — a full scenario-based check of everything you've learned in the module.
