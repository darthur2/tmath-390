Module 6 Overview

How repeated sampling turns a statistic into a distribution.

Why This Module Matters

Module 6 is about what happens when we repeat a study. A single sample gives us one sample mean, but a different random sample would have given a different value. The sampling distribution describes that sample-to-sample variability, and the central limit theorem explains why the sample mean often looks approximately normal even when the population does not. These ideas are the bridge between the probability models in Module 4, the estimation ideas in Module 5, and the confidence intervals and hypothesis tests that come next.

What You’ll Learn

You will learn how to distinguish the population distribution, the sample distribution, and the sampling distribution of the sample mean. You will see why the sampling distribution is centered at the population mean and why its standard deviation, called the standard error, equals σ/n\sigma/\sqrt n. You will study how the sample size affects the spread and shape of the sampling distribution, and you will learn when the central limit theorem justifies a normal model and when it does not. You will also practice reasoning from named probability models and from observed pilot data.

By the End of This Module, You Should Be Able To

  • Explain what a sampling distribution is and why the sample mean is a random variable.
  • Determine the mean and standard error of the sampling distribution of the sample mean.
  • Describe how the population shape and the sample size affect the shape of the sampling distribution.
  • Decide whether the central limit theorem applies, is unnecessary because the population is normal, or does not apply because the sample is too small.
  • Use a named probability model or a pilot dataset to reason about the sampling distribution.
  • Use R simulations to illustrate sampling variability and the central limit theorem.
